# Amitriptyline in IBS: an additional analysis points to more benefit mainly with psychological symptoms

An additional analysis of the earlier ATLANTIS study suggests that especially people with relatively many psychological symptoms and milder gut symptoms had more benefit from a low dose of amitriptyline. Amitriptyline is a medicine. You use it only if a doctor prescribes it.

> This is general information and not personal medical advice.

## What was studied?

Researchers afterwards divided 401 participants into four groups, based on gut symptoms and psychological symptoms. After six months they compared the fall in the symptom score (IBS-SSS) between amitriptyline and placebo. This was an exploratory, additional analysis, not a new randomised study.

## What were the findings?

In the group with mainly psychological symptoms and milder gut symptoms the score fell on average by 103 points with amitriptyline, compared with 62 points with placebo (p=0,045). Across all groups together, however, the difference between the clusters was not statistically significant (p=0,76). People with both severe gut symptoms and severe psychological symptoms stopped the study medication more often.

## What this does and does not mean

This is not proof that amitriptyline certainly works in a particular type of patient, and not a reason to start or stop this medicine yourself. The authors call the findings exploratory. Side effects and the choice of medication belong with a doctor.

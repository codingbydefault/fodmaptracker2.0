# The low-FODMAP diet has also been studied in functional dyspepsia

British researchers compared the low-FODMAP diet with ordinary dietary advice in people with functional dyspepsia: symptoms after eating, such as a full or bloated feeling in the upper abdomen. This article is background information only. It does not mean that Fodmap-Tracker is intended for dyspepsia.

> This is general information and not personal medical advice.

## What was studied?

In one hospital in the United Kingdom, 60 patients with postprandial distress syndrome received for six weeks either the low-FODMAP diet (31 people) or traditional dietary advice, such as smaller meals and less caffeine, alcohol, carbonated drinks and fatty, processed or spicy food (29 people). 63% also had IBS.

## What were the findings?

A fall of at least 0,7 points on the symptom scale (Leuven Postprandial Distress Scale) occurred in 61,3% of the low-FODMAP group, compared with 20,7% in the advice group (p<0,001). Adequate relief of the dyspepsia symptoms was reported by 71,0% versus 34,5%.

## What this does and does not mean

It is a relatively small study in one centre, over six weeks. Participants knew which dietary advice they received. Many participants also had IBS, so the result does not automatically apply to everyone with only upper-abdominal symptoms. Whether the effect lasts was not answered in this study. Decisions about diet in dyspepsia belong with a doctor or dietitian, not with an app.

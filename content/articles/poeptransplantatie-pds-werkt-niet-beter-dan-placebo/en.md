# Faecal transplant for IBS works no better than placebo

A large Norwegian study in *The Lancet* shows that a faecal transplant (FMT) for irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) worked no better than a placebo after 90 days.

> This is general information and not personal medical advice.

## What was studied?

Researchers at five hospitals in Norway randomly assigned 450 adults with moderate to severe IBS to two groups. One group received a single enema with stool from a healthy donor, the other group an enema with their own stool (placebo). Neither the participants nor the researchers knew who received what. The results of 448 participants were analysed.

## What were the findings?

After 90 days the symptom score (IBS-SSS) had fallen by at least 75 points in 40% of the people with donor stool and in 38% of the placebo group. That difference of 1,9 percentage points was not statistically significant (p=0,76). The authors write that changing the gut flora alone may not be enough to improve IBS symptoms. The share of side effects was comparable in both groups.

## What this does and does not mean

This is a large, well-designed study with a clear negative result: in this setup a single faecal transplant did not help more than placebo. It does not say that the gut flora plays no role in IBS, and it also says nothing about other ways of administering it or repeated treatments. A faecal transplant is not a standard treatment for IBS.

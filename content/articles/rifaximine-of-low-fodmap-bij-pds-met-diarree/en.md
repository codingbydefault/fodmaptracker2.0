# Rifaximin or low-FODMAP in IBS with diarrhoea: a comparable effect in a small study

In an American study, the antibiotic rifaximin and the low-FODMAP diet worked equally well against symptoms in IBS with diarrhoea. Rifaximin is a medicine. You use it only if a doctor prescribes it.

> This is general information and not personal medical advice.

## What was studied?

65 adults with IBS of the diarrhoea type received for five weeks either the low-FODMAP diet or rifaximin. Researchers at the University of Michigan looked at abdominal pain, bloating and the total symptom score (IBS-SSS). They also examined whether a breath test or certain gut bacteria could predict who would benefit.

## What were the findings?

In both groups, abdominal pain, bloating and the symptom score decreased comparably. Stool consistency did not change clearly. The breath test was an unreliable predictor. Some bacteria at the start were associated with who did or did not improve, but the authors write that this must first be confirmed before it can guide treatment choices.

## What this does and does not mean

It is a small study of five weeks. “Comparable” does not mean here that both approaches work for everyone, and it also does not mean that you can choose yourself between diet and medicine. Whether the effect lasts was not studied here.

## Sources

- [Lee, Rao, Chey et al., Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology, online 21 April 2026](https://www.cghjournal.org/article/S1542-3565(26)00316-2/abstract)

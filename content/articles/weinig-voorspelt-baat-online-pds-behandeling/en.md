# Little predicts who benefits from online IBS treatment

A Norwegian analysis in *BMC Gastroenterology* examined who benefits most from an internet treatment for IBS. Hardly any reliable predictors emerged.

> This is general information and not personal medical advice.

## What was studied?

Researchers looked at data from 350 adults from an earlier randomised study (NCT06117865). That study compared online cognitive behavioural therapy, a FODMAP module, both together, or patient education only. The main measure was a fall of at least 50 points on the symptom score (IBS-SSS). It was an exploratory, additional analysis, not the original main study.

## What were the findings?

No strong, consistent predictors were found of who clearly improves. People who had previously had little benefit from the FODMAP diet had a smaller chance of improvement. People who followed the education modules well had a greater chance of a fall of at least 50 points. The explanatory power of the statistical models was low to moderate. The authors advise against using these characteristics to predict in advance whether a treatment will work for someone.

## What this does and does not mean

This does not mean that online treatment is pointless, and it also does not mean that an earlier disappointing experience with diet is a reason to stop. It mainly underlines that a person's response is hard to predict. You can discuss earlier diet experiences with your dietitian.
